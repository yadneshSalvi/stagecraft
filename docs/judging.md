# Judge walkthrough

Open [STAGECRAFT](https://stagecraft-swart.vercel.app) and choose **Open example**. Each browser receives a private working copy. Keep the same cookies to return. THE LAST LIGHT is a fictional film; its costume and face-detail images are genuine recorded YouCam outputs over fictional artwork. One Example marker identifies the production; Evidence & limits explains provenance and practical limits.

## Read the production in looks

1. Start on the **visual continuity wall**. Follow a character across three story days rather than reading only a checklist. Nora carries the same coat motif; Mae moves from an ivory blouse to midnight, and Julian from olive to rust. Day 3 also changes Nora’s makeup and Mae’s hair-colour study.
2. Open Nora’s first look. Compare the real garment preview with **Source** or **Compare**, and the coat reference. Inspection notes preserve visible changes such as hem or closure drift. Choose **Approve appearance**, fill **Reviewer** and **Note**, then **Approve**.
3. Open Mae’s day-3 look and approve its garment as an unaffected comparison. No approval was seeded.
4. Open **Settings → Approve as example** to review the complete scheduling path quickly. Every eligible current recorded output receives its own decision attributed exactly to **Example reviewer**. Your manual decisions remain yours; unseen alternatives remain unapproved. **Settings → Manage example approvals → Remove example approvals** reverses only that action.
5. In **Schedule**, choose **Check allocation**, inspect the focused result, then **Adopt these assignments**. The initial recorded quote is £500 for 17 scene-character assignments. A single available recipe is reported as FEASIBLE. Preparation, changeover and evidence notes remain visible.

You can also open Schedule before making any decisions. **Check allocation** then shows amber **Decisions needed** and **8 of 8 looks need approval**, with **Review looks** and an explicit **Approve as example** action. Recording the sample decisions from Schedule automatically rechecks the £500 plan. A genuine overlap, unavailable required unit or hard-budget conflict stays red.

## Let an approval change the physical plan

1. From Production choose **Review a change → A garment is unavailable**, then `C-014`. Preview the impact: Nora’s two continuity looks cover scenes 01/04/07 and three calls. The miniature wall highlights the affected cells. Existing unrelated decisions are preserved.
2. The two repair cards show actual Ada previews for charcoal `C-021` and burgundy wool `C-033`, with the exact garment references inset. Their outputs are attached, but neither alternative is approved for you. The adoption action stays unavailable until the chosen alternative is admissible.
3. Choose **Compare alternatives**. Inspect both real outputs and choose **Approve alternative** for burgundy, then fill **Reviewer** and **Note** and choose **Approve**. The decision binds Ada’s capture, the exact unit/reference and output, both Nora looks and their current creative intent.
4. **Return to change review** opens a refreshed impact for the same missing coat. Choose burgundy and **Adopt this revision**. The same reviewed candidate remains attached and approved; adopting it does not invent a second approval. The completion receipt reports the actual affected looks, scenes and calls, and preserved appearance/detail decisions.
5. The impact flags old garment words still present in the direction. Edit those references inline. This clears the direction’s prior approval and makes dependent appearance decisions need review. Open the affected look, inspect the existing output and approve the revised intent; unchanged pixels do not require another render. Review any independent details flagged by the changed intent, individually or through the explicit sample-decision action.
6. Recheck allocation. Burgundy changes the fictional quote to £485. If you also approve charcoal, the cost-first search can select the £465 plan instead. Unapproved alternatives cannot win. Revoking an adopted candidate invalidates its admissibility even though that garment is now in the look’s recipe.

You can test the allocator’s creative boundary while `C-014` is still available: approve burgundy, then check allocation. The result names the original and proposed units across both Nora looks and scenes 01/04/07, and flags remaining velvet/oxblood direction references. **Adopt these assignments** stays disabled until you affirm those exact swaps. Recomputing, a changed candidate or a new revision invalidates that confirmation. **Why these units?** also retains the losing original recipe and its production-price difference; the example’s £15 difference is charged once, not once per scene. **Method** keeps the search policy available in a disclosure.

Prices are synthetic quotation facts and cost each physical unit once. Mae and Ruth have distinct ivory blouse units even though their public reference artwork is shared. An optimal mathematical assignment does not establish fitting, stock, transport or practical changeover evidence.

You can approve both coat alternatives before adopting the repair. Each repair card assesses its own exact reviewed candidate, so burgundy can remain reviewable at £485 while charcoal is also reviewable at £465. Choosing burgundy preserves its selected output and approval. A later unrestricted Schedule check may recommend charcoal; that different garment still requires explicit swap confirmation. A cheaper alternative does not by itself make the chosen repair a constraint conflict.

## Round-three repair checks

On a fresh example, leave other looks unapproved. Review C-014's unavailability and approve the burgundy alternative. The change dialog must report the remaining decisions in amber and offer **Review looks** or **Approve as example**. Recording example decisions rechecks the proposal automatically. An empty red conflict is never a substitute for missing approvals.

After returning from an alternative, reload the production. Consumed `impact`, `change`, and `recast` URL parameters must be gone, and reload must not create another proposal or revision.

When old garment wording is intentional, **Keep these words deliberately** records a named, scoped acknowledgement. It does not approve an image, establish fit, or suppress later changes to the reference. Editing the wording remains available.

## Inspect the independent face details

In a look’s studio, **Costume**, **Makeup**, **Hair colour** and **Necklace** are separate views. Each face operation uses a separately permitted capture, its own recipe and an exact-output decision. Source/result comparison never fabricates a body/face composite.

Nora’s makeup uses matte berry lip and blush, with smoothing and reshaping fixed to zero. Mae’s requested hair presets change from Chocolate Brown to Copper Red; the first rendered shade visibly reads wine/plum and the inspection note says so. Nora’s necklace uses an exact brass pendant reference. Garment substitutions preserve valid independent details. Recasting or changing the relevant face capture invalidates them.

## Brief the crew and suppliers

In **Handoff**, prepare a sourcing draft for the selected units. Requested, confirmed, received and returned are separate recorded stages with an operator and evidence. The example remains synthetic; the application sends no messages or reservations.

Choose **Export supplier orders** to inspect quotation-level rows, download CSV or print/save PDF. A multi-unit quotation is exported once, with its original status, dates, provenance and unresolved evidence. Export does not change the working book.

Create a crew pack for selected calls. It freezes the exact costume, makeup, hair and accessory evidence, unit facts and source revision. Unresolved physical evidence produces a labelled working pack. Online, **Show requirements** opens the complete readiness list; printing automatically includes it even when the list is collapsed. Change a dependency and revisit it: the online copy says **Superseded**, retaining the original snapshot. A printed copy cannot update itself.

## Bring your own script

Choose **Start your own**, import permitted text/Fountain and review the draft before adoption. Whitespace, curly quotes and forced Fountain headings are normalized for comparison only; stored source spans still point to the untouched original. Individual ungrounded cues are flagged/dropped while the good draft remains reviewable. Unknown story days stay explicit.

Use the **Example library** to add a synthetic performer and exact garment references without arranging a photo session. Select **Preview with performer** in the garment library; **Preview ready** marks verified free pairings for that exact source. Cast cards also offer **See ready garments**. Assign performers to your script characters and mark a character **Own clothes** when the department is not supplying that wardrobe.

For a complete free path, pair Ada with any ready coat, Jules with the olive or rust jacket, or Mina with a ready blouse or midnight dress. Create looks with unambiguous scene scopes. Wait for **Saved preview ready**, then choose **Create look** to attach its verified recording for inspection and approval. Choosing an unsupported pair offers actual recorded garment alternatives for the same performer first; using another performer is explicit. Trust depends on exact imported source/reference bytes and settings, not a name or a merely similar upload. Fresh generation can be resting throughout this path.

Choose **Draft calls from script**, pick the first call date/time and duration/gap, then edit the resulting calls. Story chronology stays separate from shooting order. Uploaded body and face sources have independent permissions and capture versions. JPEG, PNG and WebP are supported; HEIC/HEIF produces a clear conversion instruction.

Fresh generation is deliberate: a garment estimates 2 units; makeup, hair colour or necklace estimates 1. Defaults allow 8 fresh jobs/app/day and 4/browser/day, with a cached shared-account reserve floor of 200 units. When fresh generation rests, recorded results and saved work remain usable. **Check saved request status** performs a GET lookup; an explicit retry retains the original request identifier. An ambiguous provider submission is never automatically repeated.
