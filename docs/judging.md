Open [STAGECRAFT](https://stagecraft-swart.vercel.app) and choose **Step inside the production**. Each browser receives a private working copy. Keep the same cookies to return. THE LAST LIGHT is a fictional film; labelled YouCam images are genuine recorded provider outputs from synthetic cast and wardrobe sources.

## Read the production in looks

1. Start on the **visual continuity wall**. Follow a character across three story days rather than reading only a checklist. Nora carries the same coat motif; Mae moves from an ivory blouse to midnight, and Julian from olive to rust. Day 3 also changes Nora’s makeup and Mae’s hair-colour study.
2. Open Nora’s first look. Compare the real garment preview with **Original source** and the coat reference. Inspection notes preserve visible changes such as hem or closure drift. Record **Approve appearance** with your name/role and a rationale.
3. Open Mae’s day-3 look and approve its garment as an unaffected comparison. No approval was seeded.
4. Back on Production, explicitly **Record example decisions** to review the complete scheduling path quickly. Every eligible current recorded output receives its own decision attributed exactly to **Example reviewer (sample decision)**. Your manual decisions remain yours; unseen alternatives remain unapproved. **Manage example decisions → Remove sample decisions** reverses only that action.
5. In **Schedule**, choose **Check the allocation**, inspect the focused result, then **Adopt these assignments**. The initial recorded quote is £500 for 17 scene-character assignments. A single available recipe is reported as FEASIBLE. Preparation, changeover and evidence notes remain visible.

## Let an approval change the physical plan

1. From Production choose **Review a change → A garment is unavailable**, then `C-014`. Preview the impact: Nora’s two continuity looks cover scenes 01/04/07 and three calls. The miniature wall highlights the affected cells. Existing unrelated decisions are preserved.
2. The two repair cards show actual Ada previews for charcoal `C-021` and burgundy wool `C-033`, with the exact garment references inset. Their outputs are attached, but neither alternative is approved for you. The adoption action stays unavailable until the chosen alternative is admissible.
3. Choose **Compare & review alternatives**. Inspect both real outputs and approve burgundy with an explicit rationale. The decision binds Ada’s capture, the exact unit/reference and output, both Nora looks and their current creative intent.
4. **Return to change review**, preview the current impact again, choose burgundy and **Adopt this revision**. The same reviewed candidate remains attached and approved; adopting it does not invent a second approval.
5. The impact flags old garment words still present in the direction. Edit those references inline. This clears the direction’s prior approval and makes dependent appearance decisions need review. Open the affected look, inspect the existing output and approve the revised intent; unchanged pixels do not require another render. Review any independent details flagged by the changed intent, individually or through the explicit sample-decision action.
6. Recheck allocation. Burgundy changes the fictional quote to £485. If you also approve charcoal, the cost-first search can select the £465 plan instead. Unapproved alternatives cannot win. Revoking an adopted candidate invalidates its admissibility even though that garment is now in the look’s recipe.

Prices are synthetic quotation facts and cost each physical unit once. Mae and Ruth have distinct ivory blouse units even though their public reference artwork is shared. An optimal mathematical assignment does not establish fitting, stock, transport or practical changeover evidence.

## Inspect the independent face details

In a look’s studio, **Costume**, **Makeup detail**, **Hair colour** and **Necklace** are separate views. Each face operation uses a separately permitted capture, its own recipe and an exact-output decision. Source/result comparison never fabricates a body/face composite.

Nora’s makeup uses matte berry lip and blush, with smoothing and reshaping fixed to zero. Mae’s requested hair presets change from Chocolate Brown to Copper Red; the first rendered shade visibly reads wine/plum and the inspection note says so. Nora’s necklace uses an exact brass pendant reference. Garment substitutions preserve valid independent details. Recasting or changing the relevant face capture invalidates them.

## Brief the crew and suppliers

In **Handoff**, prepare a sourcing draft for the selected units. Requested, confirmed, received and returned are separate recorded stages with an operator and evidence. The example remains synthetic; the application sends no messages or reservations.

Open **Supplier hire orders** to inspect quotation-level rows, download CSV or print/save PDF. A multi-unit quotation is exported once, with its original status, dates, provenance and unresolved evidence. Export does not change the working book.

Create a crew pack for selected calls. It freezes the exact costume, makeup, hair and accessory evidence, unit facts and source revision. Unresolved physical evidence produces a labelled working pack. Change a dependency and revisit it: the online copy says **Superseded**, retaining the original snapshot. A printed copy cannot update itself.

## Bring your own script

Choose **Start your own**, import permitted text/Fountain and review the draft before adoption. Whitespace, curly quotes and forced Fountain headings are normalized for comparison only; stored source spans still point to the untouched original. Individual ungrounded cues are flagged/dropped while the good draft remains reviewable. Unknown story days stay explicit.

Use the **Example library** to add a synthetic performer and exact garment references without arranging a photo session. Assign performers to your script characters. Mark a character **Own clothes** when the department is not supplying that wardrobe. Create looks with unambiguous scene scopes, inspect an exact matching recorded result if available, and approve it. Identical trusted provider inputs can reuse a recording across new look IDs; a merely similar upload cannot.

Choose **Draft calls**, pick the first call date/time and duration/gap, then edit the resulting calls. Story chronology stays separate from shooting order. Uploaded body and face sources have independent permissions and capture versions. JPEG, PNG and WebP are supported; HEIC/HEIF produces a clear conversion instruction.

Fresh generation is deliberate: a garment estimates 2 units; makeup, hair colour or necklace estimates 1. Defaults allow 8 fresh jobs/app/day and 4/browser/day, with a cached shared-account reserve floor of 200 units. When fresh generation rests, recorded results and saved work remain usable. **Check saved request status** performs a GET lookup; an explicit retry retains the original request identifier. An ambiguous provider submission is never automatically repeated.
